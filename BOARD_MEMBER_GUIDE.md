# Board Member & Signer Guide

*Understand what you're committing to before saying yes*

---

## The Two Roles

### Board Member

**Decision-maker.** You attend monthly meetings, review materials, vote on major decisions.

**Responsibilities:**
- Attend board meetings (2–3 hours/month)
- Read materials before meetings (1–2 hours/month)
- Vote on: budget, patronage formula, ventures, governance changes, disputes
- Maintain confidentiality on sensitive matters
- Act in the cooperative's interest (fiduciary duty)
- Declare conflicts of interest

**Time commitment:** 5–10 hours/month in early years, settling to 3–4 hours/month as processes mature.

**Legal responsibility:** You have a duty to act carefully and in good faith. Covered by Directors & Officers insurance in case of reasonable mistakes.

**Decision authority:** High. Your vote counts equally with other board members on all major decisions.

---

### Signer

**Executor.** You sign documents the board has approved.

**Responsibilities:**
- Sign contracts, agreements, and financial documents authorized by the board
- Maintain records of what you signed and when
- Never sign without board approval
- Understand what you're signing before you sign it

**Time commitment:** Low (5–10 signatures/month in early stage).

**Legal responsibility:** Moderate. You represent the cooperative when signing. Protected by board approval process and D&O insurance if you follow procedures.

**Decision authority:** None. You execute decisions the board makes; you don't make them.

---

## Can You Be Both?

Yes. In early-stage cooperatives, board members often sign documents too. This is efficient and common.

---

## What You're NOT Responsible For

- Employee actions (the cooperative is liable, not you personally)
- Cooperative debts (unless you personally guaranteed them)
- Decisions made by other board members
- Acts of previous boards
- Decisions you voted against (if the board decided otherwise)

---

## First 3 Years: What Actually Happens

### Year 1: Foundation

Board focuses on: basic operations, hiring stewards, defining patronage formula, approving first ventures.

Signings: leases, vendor contracts, tax forms, bank accounts.

**Intensity:** High. Monthly 3-hour meetings + 3–5 hours prep/discussion.

### Year 2: Growth

Board focuses on: reviewing patronage formula, approving new ventures, evaluating performance, resolving early conflicts.

Signings: venture contracts, patronage allocations, quarterly tax filings.

**Intensity:** Moderate. Monthly 2–3 hour meetings + 1–2 hours prep.

### Year 3: Optimization

Board focuses on: refining governance, long-term planning, preparing next growth phase.

Signings: ongoing contracts, annual filings, venture updates.

**Intensity:** Sustainable. Monthly 2-hour meetings + 1 hour prep.

## Red Flags: Questions Before Committing

**For board members:**

- Do I have 5–10 hours/month consistently? (Undercommitment hurts the board.)
- Can I attend meetings reliably?
- Am I willing to be bound by majority decisions, even if I voted no?
- Do I have any conflicts of interest with ventures or funding?

**For signers:**

- Will I understand what I'm signing before I sign?
- Am I comfortable with legal responsibility?
- Will I check with the board before signing anything?

---

## How It Works in Practice

**Good process:**
1. Board discusses and votes approval
2. Operations lead negotiates terms
3. Board reviews final version
4. Signer verifies it matches approval and signs
5. Document is legally binding

**Bad process to avoid:**
- Signer signs without board input
- Board makes decisions without checking feasibility
- No clear record of who approved what

---

## Protection: D&O Insurance

The cooperative maintains Directors & Officers insurance covering:
- Personal liability for board decisions made in good faith
- Errors in judgment (as long as you acted reasonably)
- Good-faith mistakes in governance

**What's NOT covered:**
- Criminal conduct or fraud
- Gross negligence or reckless behavior
- Personal conflicts of interest you conceal

In a well-run cooperative, actual liability is very rare.

---

## Decision Checklist

Before committing, confirm:

- [ ] I understand the time commitment for my role
- [ ] I can attend meetings reliably
- [ ] I'm comfortable with the legal responsibility
- [ ] I have no undisclosed conflicts of interest
- [ ] I'm willing to act in the cooperative's interest, not personal interest
- [ ] I understand I'm protected by D&O insurance for good-faith actions

---

## What to Say When Committing

If you're ready:

> "I understand that this role requires [5–10 hours/month for board] [minimal time for signer]. I have that capacity, I'm comfortable with the legal responsibility, and I'm ready to contribute to cooperative governance."

If you need more information:

> "I want to understand [meeting frequency / time commitment / specific responsibility] before I commit. Can we discuss?"

---

## Key Documents to Review

- Cooperative bylaws (defines board powers and procedures)
- Operating agreement (day-to-day governance)
- Patronage formula (how contributions create value)
- D&O insurance policy (coverage and limits)
