The Chain

A convergence
that never ends

ETHBoulder was a bounded convergence — a 3-day event captured in the knowledge graph. Techne is a perpetual convergence: an ongoing, append-only chain where cooperative economic events accumulate over time. There is no closing ceremony. The chain grows with every contribution.

append-only

Merkle chain

Every entry is hash-linked to its predecessor. No edits, no deletes. Corrections create new entries. The chain is the audit trail — verifiable from genesis to now.

natural language

Say what you did

"Spent 3 hours on the treasury API." The system extracts a typed contribution, classifies it against the patronage formula, and queues it for approval. Five chain entries from one sentence.

IRC 1385

Patronage formula

At period close, surplus is allocated to members based on weighted contributions. The formula execution itself becomes a chain entry — you can see exactly how every allocation was computed.

704(b)

Compliance on chain

The system records not just what happened, but what it checked. Double-entry verification, substantial economic effect tests, and allocation invariants — all chain entries.

computed

Capital accounts

Balances aren't stored — they're computed from the chain. Beginning balance + contributions + allocations − distributions = ending balance. State is a view over events.

on-chain anchoring

Integrity proof

The chain lives off-chain in Supabase. Periodically, the head hash is anchored on Base — a public, timestamped proof that the chain hasn't been tampered with.

Composable Tools

Three tools,
one chain

The patronage system is built from three independent, event-sourced tools. They communicate through the chain, not shared databases. Each tool writes entries; the chain connects them.

Treasury

Double-entry accounting, capital accounts, balance computation, and reporting. Every transaction posts a chain entry with matching debits and credits. Balances are computed views — never stored, always derived.

double-entry capital accounts balance sheets K-1 assembly

People

Member identity, contribution lifecycle, valuation, and approval workflows. Each contribution moves through five stages — submit, validate, value, approve, credit — and each transition is a chain entry.

member identity contributions approval workflow NL parsing

Agreements

Patronage formulas, period close, allocation calculation, distribution scheduling, and governance approval. The formula weights are the cooperative's value statement expressed as arithmetic.

Contribution Lifecycle

From sentence
to capital account

Every contribution moves through five stages. Each stage transition is an entry in the chain. One sentence generates a verifiable, auditable trail from natural language to capital account credit.

1

Submit

Member describes their contribution in natural language. "Facilitated the Wednesday standup" or "deposited $500 operating capital." The NL parser extracts type, duration, and context.

2

Validate

System checks: is this a recognized contribution type? Does the member have standing? Is the period open? Validation passes or returns for clarification.

3

Value

Contribution receives a monetary value. Labor is valued at the member's rate × hours. Cash at face value. Revenue at amount received. Community involvement at the cooperative's standard rate.

4

Approve

A steward reviews and approves. Approval creates a chain entry linking the approver to the contribution. This is the governance checkpoint — no contribution credits without human review.

5

Credit

Treasury posts a double-entry transaction: debit the expense category, credit the member's capital account. The balance updates automatically — computed from the chain, not stored.

│
#43 people.contribution.submitted → type: labor, member: 008
│
#44 people.contribution.valued → hours: 3, rate: $75, value: $225
│
#45 people.contribution.approved → approvedBy: steward
│
#46 treasury.transaction.posted → debit: labor_expense, credit: capital_008
Design Framework

Seven layers

Every information system decomposes into a progressive set of design patterns. Each layer depends on the ones beneath it. The stack reads bottom-up, like sedimentary rock. Each chain entry is tagged with its layer — making the graph filterable by abstraction level.

1

Identity

Distinguishing one thing from another. Member UUIDs, member numbers, ENS names, account codes, transaction IDs. Without identity, nothing else is possible.

2

State

Recording attributes of identifiable things. Member profiles, account balances, contribution amounts, operating agreement versions. Capital accounts are computed state — derived from events.

3

Relationship

Connecting identifiable things. REA ontology — Event connects Resource to Agent. Member-to-contribution links. Parent-child accounts. Allocation-to-member.

4

Event

Recording that something happened. ContributionApproved, TransactionPosted, PeriodClosed. Events are immutable facts — the atoms of the chain.

5

Flow

Value moving between agents. Patronage allocation: surplus flows to members proportional to contribution. Capital account credits and debits. The patronage formula is a flow specification.

6

Constraint

Rules governing valid states. IRC 704(b) compliance. Double-entry invariant: debits must equal credits. Formula weights must have documented business purpose. Cash distribution rate ≥ 20%.

7

View

Presenting information for a purpose. Member allocation statements, balance sheets, K-1 tax forms, the patronage graph. Views make the chain legible to humans and accountants.

The weights are the cooperative's value statement expressed as arithmetic. A development shop might weight labor at 60%. A sales cooperative might weight revenue at 50%. The only requirement: the weights must have documented business purpose, satisfying the IRS substantiality test.
