The Studio

Infrastructure,
not product

Techne is a venture studio organized as a cooperative. We provide the infrastructure — legal structure, shared space, planning, capital access — for autonomous ventures. We are soil, not plant.

As a Colorado Limited Cooperative Association, ownership isn't static. It's earned through patronage — your actual contribution during each period. Work more, contribute more, bring in more revenue? Your share reflects that, period by period.

This requires accounting infrastructure that most cooperatives don't have. So we built it.

The Approach

The chain
is the ledger

Every economic event in the cooperative's life — a contribution logged, a period closed, an allocation approved — becomes an immutable, hash-linked entry in an append-only chain. Capital accounts aren't stored in a database. They're computed from the chain.

append-only

Immutable record

Every entry is hash-linked to its predecessor. No edits, no deletes. Corrections create new entries. The chain is the audit trail — verifiable from the first entry to the latest.

natural language

Say what you did

"Spent 3 hours on the treasury API." The system extracts a typed contribution, classifies it against the patronage formula, and queues it for approval. Five chain entries from one sentence.

IRC 1385

Patronage formula

At period close, surplus is allocated to members based on weighted contributions. The formula execution itself becomes a chain entry — every allocation is transparent and reproducible.

704(b)

Compliance as data

The system records not just what happened, but what it checked. Double-entry verification, substantial economic effect tests, and allocation invariants — all entries in the chain.

derived state

Capital accounts

Balances aren't stored — they're computed. Beginning balance + contributions + allocations − distributions = ending balance. State is a view over events, not a separate data store.

integrity

On-chain anchoring

The chain lives off-chain for speed and cost. Periodically, the head hash is anchored on-chain — a public, timestamped proof that the record hasn't been tampered with.

Composable Tools

Three tools,
one chain

The patronage system is built from three independent, event-sourced tools. They communicate through the chain, not shared databases. Each tool writes entries; the chain connects them.

Treasury

Double-entry accounting, capital accounts, balance computation, and reporting. Every transaction posts a chain entry with matching debits and credits. Balances are computed views — never stored, always derived.

People

Member identity, contribution lifecycle, valuation, and approval workflows. Each contribution moves through five stages — submit, validate, value, approve, credit — and each transition is a chain entry.

Agreements

Patronage formulas, period close, allocation calculation, distribution scheduling, and governance approval. The formula weights are the cooperative's value statement expressed as arithmetic.

Contribution Lifecycle

From sentence
to capital account

Every contribution moves through five stages. Each stage transition creates an entry in the chain. One sentence generates a verifiable trail from natural language to capital account credit.

1

Submit

A member describes their contribution in plain language. "Facilitated the Wednesday standup" or "deposited $500 operating capital." A parser extracts the type, duration, and context.

2

Validate

Is this a recognized contribution type? Does the member have standing? Is the period open? Validation passes or returns for clarification.

3

Value

The contribution receives a monetary value. Labor is valued at the member's rate × hours. Cash at face value. Revenue at amount received. Community involvement at the cooperative's standard rate.

4

Approve

A steward reviews and approves. The approval entry links the approver to the contribution. This is the governance checkpoint — no contribution credits without human review.

5

Credit

Treasury posts a double-entry transaction: debit the expense category, credit the member's capital account. The balance updates — computed from the chain, not stored.

Design Framework

Seven layers

Every information system decomposes into a progressive set of design patterns. Each layer depends on the ones beneath it. The stack reads bottom-up, like sedimentary rock. Each chain entry is tagged with its layer.

1

Identity

Distinguishing one thing from another. Member numbers, account codes, transaction IDs. Without identity, nothing else is possible.

2

State

Recording attributes of identifiable things. Member profiles, account balances, contribution amounts. Capital accounts are derived state — computed from events, not stored directly.

3

Relationship

Connecting identifiable things. Member-to-contribution links. Parent-child accounts. Allocation-to-member. The REA ontology: Event connects Resource to Agent.

4

Event

Recording that something happened. ContributionApproved, TransactionPosted, PeriodClosed. Events are immutable facts — the atoms of the chain.

5

Flow

Value moving between agents. Surplus flows to members proportional to contribution. The patronage formula is a flow specification — it describes how value moves.

6

Constraint

Rules governing valid states. Debits must equal credits. Cash distribution rate ≥ 20%. Formula weights must have documented business purpose.

7

View

Presenting information for a purpose. Member statements, balance sheets, K-1 tax forms, the patronage graph. Views make the chain legible to humans and accountants.

The weights are the cooperative's value statement expressed as arithmetic. A development shop might weight labor at 60%. A sales cooperative might weight revenue at 50%. The only requirement: the weights must have documented business purpose, satisfying the IRS substantiality test.
