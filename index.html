The Root

Identity is
the first layer

In any information system, identity precedes everything. Before you can record state, form relationships, track events, or create views — you must first be able to distinguish one thing from another. commons.id is that act of distinguishing, applied to everything the commons holds.

Ideas get addresses

Every artifact in the knowledge graph resolves to a permanent URL. A proposal born in a 2026 conversation is still findable in 2030. Ideas become citable, linkable, alive.

People get threads

Participants carry a persistent identity across convergence events. Your contributions, your connections, your commitments — one thread, woven through many gatherings.

Events get memory

Each convergence is a named, locatable moment in time — with its own knowledge graph, its own artifacts, its own ecology. Identity gives events continuity.

Agents get roles

AI agents that observe, extract, and synthesize are first-class participants with their own identities, capabilities, and accountability within the commons.

Why Identity Matters

Without identity,
the energy dissipates

Convergence events generate extraordinary moments — new relationships, emergent ideas, shared commitments. Then the gathering ends. The problem is not that energy fades. The problem is that nothing had a name.

Design Framework

e/H-LAM/T

Douglas Engelbart understood that human capability emerges from the co-evolution of five elements. We extend his framework with an ecological foundation — the e/ layer. Identity is the thread that runs through all six: you cannot name what you cannot distinguish.

Ecology

All coordination happens in a place. Every convergence carries bioregional identity — watershed, elevation, season. The land is not backdrop; it is context that shapes what's possible.

  • Bioregion identity per convergence
  • Seasonal and ecological metadata
  • Place as first-class dimension

Human

Participants with persistent identity across events. Not users — practitioners with embodied knowledge, relationships, and unique vantage points that deepen over time.

  • commons.id/p/{name} — your thread
  • Interest graphs and social fabric
  • Personal sense-making + collective synthesis

Language

How we name things shapes what we can coordinate around. The knowledge graph schema is a shared vocabulary — tags, dimensions, relationship types — that gives the commons a grammar.

  • Ontology for artifacts and relationships
  • Tags as shared vocabulary
  • Translation between technical, civic, ecological frames

Artifacts

The durable traces of human coordination. Each artifact carries origin context, lineage, and stewards. Because they have identity (a permanent URL), they can be cited, linked, evolved.

  • commons.id/a/{id} — permanent address
  • State lifecycle: seed → active → pattern
  • The graph is the master artifact

Methodology

Three temporal states. Agent orchestration roles. Stewardship practices. The rhythm of convergence and continuation — methodology is the gardening, not just the garden.

  • Pre / During / Post event states
  • Agent roles: observer, synthesizer, steward
  • Commitment tracking as methodology

Training

How the system learns. Pattern recognition across convergences. The bootstrap discipline: using the commons to improve the commons. Identity enables learning — you can't improve what you can't name.

  • Pattern extraction across events
  • Onboarding as identity creation
  • The commons improves itself

Foundations

Principles

Identity precedes everything

Before state, before relationships, before events — you must first distinguish one thing from another. Naming is the first act of care. commons.id is that act, applied to the commons.

Context integrity

Preserve not just content but the conditions under which it emerged — the relationships, the energy, the place, the moment. Without context, artifacts are just data.

Artifacts over archives

A living commons, not a static repository. Artifacts evolve, fork, connect, and sometimes gracefully fade. The graph is alive. Every artifact has identity; not every identity persists.

Grounded in place

Every convergence happens somewhere. The ecological layer carries identity too — watershed, elevation, season. Technology that forgets its ground extracts. Technology that remembers can contribute.

Amplifying aliveness

Technology should amplify what is most alive in us. The commons carries forward the generative energy of human gathering — not by capturing it, but by giving it names that endure.

Open by default

The commons is a shared resource. What is light should be cosmo — patterns and identities that flow freely across communities. What is heavy stays local. The namespace is open.
