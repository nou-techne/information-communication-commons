The energy dissipates

Convergence events generate extraordinary moments — new relationships, emergent ideas, shared commitments. Then the gathering ends, and the context scatters.

  • The heart-mind space present when an idea emerged — gone
  • Connections between sessions, between people, between years — invisible
  • Commitments made in high-energy moments — untracked
  • Patterns spanning multiple gatherings — undiscovered
  • Same conversations repeat, year after year

What survives is scattered across personal notes, forgotten channels, fading memory. The commons has no memory. Until now.

Design Framework

e/H-LAM/T — the augmentation system

Douglas Engelbart understood that human capability doesn't live in any single tool or person — it emerges from the co-evolution of five elements, grounded in the living systems that make all of it possible.

We extend Engelbart's H-LAM/T framework with an ecological foundation — the e/ layer — recognizing that all human coordination occurs within living systems, not above them. This framework anchors every dimension of the commons: how we structure data, how we present information, and how we build features.

e/ — Ecology

The living ground

All coordination happens in a place, in a season, in a watershed. The ecological layer grounds the commons in the bioregional context where convergence occurs — the land, the water, the climate patterns, the relationships between species. Technology that forgets its ground extracts. Technology that remembers its ground can contribute.

In the commons: Bioregional context for each convergence. Seasonal rhythms. The land as participant, not backdrop. Artifacts carry place — where they emerged, what the ground was doing, what the watershed needs.

H — Human

The practitioners

People and their evolving capabilities. Not users — participants. Each person brings embodied knowledge, relationships, and a unique vantage point on the questions the commons holds.

In the commons: Participant profiles. Interest graphs. The social fabric that connects people across events. Personal sense-making alongside collective synthesis.

L — Language

The shared vocabulary

How we name things shapes what we can coordinate around. Language includes formal ontologies, informal framings, metaphors, and the memetic structures that carry ideas between contexts.

In the commons: The knowledge graph schema. Ontologies for artifacts, relationships, and events. The naming conventions that make the commons navigable. Translation between vocabularies (technical, civic, ecological).

A — Artifacts

What persists

The durable traces of human coordination — documents, software, recordings, proposals, patterns, and the knowledge graph itself. Artifacts are how the commons has memory.

In the commons: Ideas, proposals, commitments, syntheses, patterns. Each artifact carries origin context, lineage, stewards, and dimensions of meaning. The knowledge graph is the master artifact.

M — Methodology

How we work together

The practices, protocols, and workflows that turn individual capability into collective coordination. Methodology is how the commons is tended — the gardening, not just the garden.

In the commons: Three temporal states (pre-event, during, post-event). Artifact lifecycle management. Agent orchestration roles. Stewardship practices. The rhythm of convergence and continuation.

T — Training

Transformation through practice

Developing genuine capability — in participants, in agents, in the collective. Training is how the system improves itself, how practitioners deepen, how the commons becomes more alive over time.

In the commons: Onboarding for new participants. Agent capability development. Pattern recognition across convergences. The bootstrap discipline: using the commons to improve the commons.
Change one element, the others shift. Develop all together, possibility expands. The framework is not a checklist — it's a lens for seeing how capability emerges from relationship.

Three states — the rhythm

The commons spans three temporal states around each convergence event. Artifacts flow between states, accumulating context and deepening meaning.

Pre-Event

Listening · Orienting · Preparing

Participants register intent and interests. Agents surface relevant prior artifacts — threads from previous convergences, ongoing work, emerging questions. Personal agents prepare context packages: "Here's what's alive in the commons relevant to you."

  • Participant profiles and interest graphs
  • Question threads that want to be explored
  • Context packages curated by personal agents
  • Prior artifacts surfaced by relevance

During Event

Active · Emergent · Co-creative

Conversations feed directly into the knowledge graph. Agents extract entities, relationships, proposals, and commitments in real time. Live summaries replace raw chat streams. Cross-session connections surface naturally.

  • Conversation transcription and entity extraction
  • Live summaries and cross-session connections
  • Commitment tracking as they're spoken
  • Emergent themes across concurrent sessions

Post-Event

Integration · Synthesis · Continuation

The living archive receives feedback and evolves. Commitments are tracked against progress. Agents continue coordination. Themes deepen through ongoing conversation and work. A proposal becomes a project becomes a pattern.

  • Synthesis documents (agent-generated, human-reviewed)
  • Commitment progress tracking
  • Pattern extraction across multiple convergences
  • The commons grows between events

For agents and humans

The knowledge graph is the commons. Two interfaces into the same living structure.

Agent API

A structured service for agent coordination, observation, and artifact exchange. Clear roles: orchestrator, observer, synthesizer, representative, steward. Structured knowledge, not raw chat streams.

Agents query artifacts, submit observations, track commitments, and surface connections. The API speaks the language of the knowledge graph — entities, relationships, events, dimensions.

Human Platform

A platform designed to feel like exploring a living garden, not querying a database. Browse by theme, person, time, or energy. Progressive disclosure — start with summaries, dive into detail.

Contribution is natural. Add reflections, corrections, new connections. Voice-friendly. Your agent assists with tagging and linking. See your own thread through the commons alongside the whole.

Principles

Context integrity

Preserve not just content but the conditions under which it emerged — the relationships, the energy, the place. Without context, artifacts are just data.

Artifacts over archives

A living commons, not a static repository. Artifacts evolve, fork, connect, and sometimes fade. The graph is alive.

Amplifying aliveness

Technology should amplify what is most alive in us. The commons exists to carry forward the generative energy of human gathering, not to extract or reduce it.

Grounded in place

Every convergence happens somewhere. The ecological layer — watershed, season, bioregion — is not background. It's context that shapes what's possible and what's needed.

Open by default

The commons is a shared resource. Patterns, protocols, and ideas flow freely. What is light should be cosmo. What is heavy stays local.

The commons is forming

First convergence: ETHBoulder 2026 (February 13–16, Boulder, Colorado). The knowledge graph begins recording. Artifacts start accumulating. The commons comes alive.
