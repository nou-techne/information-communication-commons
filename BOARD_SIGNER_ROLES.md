# Board Member & Signer Roles — RegenHub, LCA

*What you're actually agreeing to in the first 3 years*

---

## Quick Version

**Board Member:** You help make decisions about how the cooperative runs and allocates resources. You have legal authority and responsibility.

**Signer:** You can sign documents on behalf of the cooperative (contracts, tax forms, etc.). You represent the cooperative legally when signing.

These can be the same person or different people. You might be one, both, or neither.

---

## What is a Signer?

### The Role

A **signer** is someone authorized to sign legal documents on behalf of RegenHub, LCA. When you sign something as a representative, the cooperative is bound by that signature.

### Specific Responsibilities

- **Contracts & agreements** — sign vendor agreements, leases, service contracts
- **Bank documents** — authorize wire transfers, open accounts, reconcile statements
- **Tax documents** — IRS forms (EIN registration, annual filings, W2s)
- **Legal notices** — respond to official correspondence
- **Patronage allocations** — sign off on member payouts and capital account adjustments

### Time Commitment

**Low** — only when signing is needed (typically 5-10 documents/month in early stage).

### Legal Risk

**Moderate** — you're acting as the cooperative's agent. If you sign something that violates law or bylaws, you could be personally liable. But this is protected by:
- Clear approval process (board approval before you sign)
- Insurance (the cooperative maintains a D&O policy)
- Good faith protection (if you're following the board's direction)

### Decision-Making Authority

**None** — you don't make the decisions. The **board** makes decisions; you execute them by signing.

Exception: Emergency signing (if the board authorizes you to sign certain things without case-by-case approval).

### First 3 Years Context

In early stage:
- Fewer documents than mature cooperatives
- Most signings are operational (not existential)
- You're signing to enable operations, not strategic decisions
- The board will be very involved in what gets signed

## What is a Board Member?

### The Role

A **board member** is a decision-maker. You attend meetings, discuss cooperatives matters, and vote on:
- Annual budget
- Patronage formula
- New ventures
- Governance changes
- Disputes and exceptions
- Strategic direction

### Specific Responsibilities

#### Mandatory

1. **Attend board meetings** — typically monthly (2-3 hours)
2. **Review materials** — read agenda, documents, proposals before meetings
3. **Participate in decisions** — vote on significant matters
4. **Maintain confidentiality** — some discussions are private (personnel, sensitive ventures)
5. **Act in good faith** — prioritize cooperative interests, not personal benefit
6. **Declare conflicts** — disclose if you have personal interest in a decision
7. **Fiduciary duty** — you're a legal representative and must act with care and diligence

#### Expected (but not legally required)

- Contribute expertise (finance, law, operations, etc.)
- Mentor other stewards
- Represent the cooperative publicly
- Help resolve disputes
- Champion specific initiatives

### Time Commitment

**Moderate to High** — estimate 5-10 hours/month:
- Meetings: 2-3 hours
- Reading/prep: 1-2 hours
- Ad-hoc discussions: 2-5 hours
- First few years may be higher (learning curve)

### Legal Risk

**Moderate** — you have **fiduciary duty** (legal obligation to act in cooperative's interest). This means:
- You could be held responsible if the cooperative suffers financial loss due to your negligence or misconduct
- Protected by: D&O insurance, business judgment rule (courts defer to board decisions if made in good faith), indemnification clause in bylaws
- Realistic: In a well-run cooperative, this risk is very low

### Decision-Making Authority

**High** — you have a vote on major decisions. In RegenHub's first few years:
- Board will likely have 5-9 members (depending on bylaws)
- Major decisions require board approval
- In emergency, may require only 2-3 board members (depending on bylaws)
- Your vote counts equally with others

### First 3 Years Context

In early stage, the board's work is foundational:
- Hiring key roles (operations steward, finance steward, etc.)
- Setting patronage formula (how contributions convert to value)
- Approving first ventures
- Resolving conflicts as they arise
- Defining org culture and values
- Building governance infrastructure

This is **high-impact** but also **high-learning** work.

## Can You Be Both?

**Yes, and you probably will be.**

In the first 3 years of a cooperative, board members often sign documents too. This is efficient and common.

However, it's useful to keep them conceptually separate:
- **Board role:** decide what to do
- **Signer role:** execute what the board decided

---

## What You're NOT Responsible For

Even as a board member, you're **not** personally liable for:

- Employee actions (the cooperative is liable, not you personally)
- Debts of the cooperative (unless you personally guaranteed them)
- Decisions made by other board members (except if you voted for them and negligently failed to object)
- Acts of previous boards (you inherit the position, not the liability)

The whole point of a cooperative structure is that **the organization has legal responsibility**, not individual members.

## The First 3 Years: What Actually Happens

### Year 1: Foundation

**Board priorities:**
- Establish operations (office, systems, basic processes)
- Hire operations steward
- Define patronage formula
- Approve first ventures
- Build communication channels

**Signer needs:**
- Lease agreements
- Vendor contracts (internet, accounting software, etc.)
- Tax forms (IRS filing, Colorado annual report)
- Bank account setup

**Time:** Heaviest year. Monthly board meetings (3 hours), plus 3-5 hours prep/discussion.

### Year 2: Growth

**Board priorities:**
- Review patronage formula (does it work?)
- Approve new ventures as they emerge
- Evaluate stewards (formal review)
- Update bylaws if needed
- Resolve early conflicts

**Signer needs:**
- Venture contracts
- Patronage allocation documents
- Quarterly tax filings
- Loan or capital agreements (if needed)

**Time:** Settling into rhythm. 1-2 hours prep + 2-3 hour meeting monthly.

### Year 3: Optimization

**Board priorities:**
- Fine-tune governance (what's working, what's not?)
- Plan 3-5 year strategy
- Consider new membership classes
- Evaluate overall performance
- Mentor next generation of stewards

**Signer needs:**
- Ongoing contracts
- Annual tax filings
- Venture amendments
- Membership agreement templates

**Time:** Stabilized. 1 hour prep + 2 hour meeting monthly.

## Red Flags: Questions to Ask Before Saying Yes

**If you're considering being a board member:**

- Do I have 5-10 hours/month? (Be honest. Undercommitment hurts the board.)
- Can I attend meetings reliably? (Chronic absences undermine governance.)
- Do I understand cooperative economics? (Be willing to learn, but it helps to be curious.)
- Can I disagree with other board members respectfully? (Conflict is normal; contempt is not.)
- Am I willing to be bound by majority decisions? (Even if I voted no.)
- Do I have any conflicts of interest? (Related to ventures, funding, employment?)

**If you're considering being a signer:**

- Do I understand what I'm authorizing? (Never sign something you don't understand.)
- Have I checked with the board? (Signers should not act independently.)
- Am I comfortable with this level of legal responsibility? (D&O insurance helps, but it's real.)
- Can I maintain good records? (Especially for money movements.)

## How Board & Signer Roles Work Together

### Ideal Process

1. **Board decides** — "We should sign a contract with Vendor X for $5K/month."
2. **Board approves** — votes yes, directs operations steward to negotiate
3. **Terms negotiated** — operations steward works with vendor on details
4. **Board reviews** — sees final contract, votes approval
5. **Signer executes** — signer reads, confirms it matches board approval, signs
6. **Executed** — contract is legally binding on the cooperative

### Bad Process (to avoid)

- Signer approves things without board input
- Board decides things without asking operations steward for feasibility
- Signer signs things the board didn't approve
- No record of who approved what

## Insurance & Protection

RegenHub maintains:

- **D&O (Directors & Officers) insurance** — protects board members from personal liability
- **General liability** — protects against injury/damage claims
- **Errors & omissions** — protects against professional mistakes

This means: you're protected against most reasonable risks. Catastrophic personal liability is unlikely.

**However:** Insurance doesn't cover criminal conduct, fraud, or gross negligence. If you act recklessly or dishonestly, you lose protection.

## Sample Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Approving a $50K Venture

**What happens:**
1. Founder pitches venture to board
2. Board discusses fit, risk, resource needs
3. Board votes: yes or no
4. If yes: approved ventures get added to steward's portfolio
5. Signer may need to: sign venture agreement, authorize funding transfer

**Your responsibility:**
- As board member: understand the venture, ask questions, vote thoughtfully
- As signer: don't authorize something the board didn't approve

---

### Scenario 2: Monthly Patronage Allocation

**What happens:**
1. Finance steward calculates member patronage based on formula
2. Board reviews calculations, confirms formula was applied correctly
3. Board votes approval
4. Signer authorizes transfer to member accounts

**Your responsibility:**
- As board member: understand patronage formula, verify steward did math right
- As signer: verify the board approved before signing authorization

---

### Scenario 3: Conflict Between Two Ventures

**What happens:**
1. Two ventures want same resource (space, person, budget)
2. Operations steward can't resolve
3. Board hears both sides
4. Board votes to allocate to one (or restructure to both)
5. Losing venture adapts or leaves

**Your responsibility:**
- As board member: listen fairly, understand tradeoffs, vote in cooperative's interest (not personal preference)
- As signer: implement whatever the board decided

## FAQ

**Q: What if I disagree with a board decision?**
A: You vote against it. If it passes anyway, you implement it (unless it's illegal). You can resign from the board if you can't accept the direction, but you don't have veto power.

**Q: What if something goes wrong and the cooperative gets sued?**
A: The cooperative defends itself. If you acted in good faith and followed procedures, the D&O insurance covers you. If you acted negligently or fraudulently, you could be personally liable.

**Q: Can I get out of being a board member?**
A: Yes. You can resign. Typical notice is 30 days, to allow replacement.

**Q: How long is the term?**
A: Typically 2-3 years (depending on bylaws). After the term, the board can re-elect you or choose someone new.

**Q: What if I miss meetings?**
A: It depends on bylaws, but typically 2-3 unexcused absences in a row = automatic removal.

**Q: Can I be fired from the board?**
A: Yes, but not easily. Typical cause is: gross negligence, fraud, or chronic absence. The removal requires a membership or board vote.

**Q: Do I get paid?**
A: Typically no, for board service. This is volunteer governance. However, if you also work as an operations steward or other role, you do get paid for that work.

## What to Say to Todd/Savannah

When you're ready to commit, you can say something like:

> "I understand that as a board member, I'm responsible for attending monthly meetings (~3 hours) and helping make decisions about patronage, ventures, and governance. As a signer, I'll execute documents the board approves. I have the time to do this well, and I'm comfortable with the legal responsibility covered by D&O insurance. I'm ready to contribute."

Or, if you're not sure:

> "I want to be involved, but I need to understand the time commitment better. Can we talk about what actual meeting prep looks like, and what happens if I miss one?"

The key: **Be honest about your capacity and commitment level.** The cooperative needs people who say yes because they're ready, not because they feel obligated.
